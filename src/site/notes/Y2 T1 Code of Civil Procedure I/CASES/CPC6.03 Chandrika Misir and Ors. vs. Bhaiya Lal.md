Facts:
- Plots purchased in Markandey's name (uncle of plaintiffs).
- Recorded in widow Jagdamba's name after his death.
- After Jagdamba's death, plaintiffs claimed title, claiming that respondents were interfering with possession and wanted a permanent injunction and possession. 
- Plea of defendants: limitation.
- Munsif: suit barred by limitation.
- 1st Appeal: Additional Civil Judge (Varanasi): not barred by limitation, possession in favour of plaintiffs.
- 2nd Appeal: HC: limitation could not be decided till two issues were settled:
	- Found that Chandrika Misir was a minor in 1948 when Jagdamba died, and attained majority in 1955.
	- Took actual possession in 1951-52.
	- Had to decide whether the suit filed in 1955 was filed in time. 
	- Decided that the Limitation Act's period (12 yrs) would not apply, rather the UP Zamindari Abolition Rules Act would instead. (2 years from date of vesting under the act).

Issues:
- Whether the suit was barred by limitation?
- Whether the trial court lacked subject matter jurisdiction?

Ratio:
- UP Act made it clear that a suit for the ejectment of a person illegally occupying land without title should be filed in the court of the Assistant Collector (1st class) — had original jurisdiction. Hence, the suit could not be filed in a civil court.
- s. 331 cpc
- Due to lack of subject matter jurisdiction, the appeal was dismissed, since such a lack is fatal in any stage, even if the defendant did not contest it (such as in this case).
