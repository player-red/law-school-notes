By [[Y1 T1 SOC Society/PEOPLE/Pierre Bourdieu\|Pierre Bourdieu]], Chapter 3 in The Logic of Practice, pp. 52-65.

- Objectivism: the world is a spectacle offered to an observer who has their own point of view and proceeds by using principles and interactions as if they were purely symbolic exchanges.
- Theory of practice as practice: objects of knowledge and constructed and not passively recorded, and the principle of this construction is the system of structuring dispositions (habitus) which is developed in practice and is oriented towards practical functions.
	- Opposes positivist materialism for the former and intellectualist idealism for the latter.
- To escape the realism of structure you have to use ==practice== (to avoid subjectivism).
	- Practice is where the products of historical practice, and structures and habitus are found.

- Habitus is created by certain conditions of existence (family background, education, economic class) and their conditioning (behaviour trained into you).
- Habitus = systems of durable and transposable ==dispositions==.
	- They function as structures (in the individual) which tend to affect the development of other structures in society.
	- The individual uses it as a set of principles to organize practice and representations.
	- They are regulated and 'regular': without any set of explicit rules, they govern individual behaviour and lead to collective action without any organizer calling for it.
	- Eg: use of common spaces like washrooms in hostels - there are a set of rules everyone is expected to follow which aren't explicit, they are implied (ie flushing the toilet), everyone is expected to know them and does somewhat follow them collectively without someone explicitly asking them to.
	- Habitus is mostly unconscious (quote: "without presupposing a conscious aiming at ends or an express mastery of the operations necessary").
		- Eg: everyone flushes the toilet without really thinking much about it, no one needs to take a special course on how to flush it (atp some do. the horrors of yamuna good lord)
- Performance of habitus can include strategic conscious calculations but the responses that use these calculations are created by the habitus, in relation to a future possibility.
- Stimuli only work when an individual is conditioned to recognize them. (can't flush a toilet if you don't know how)
- Dispositions created by the conditions of an individual's existence are pre-adapted to their aspirations and demands.
- Expectations generated by the habitus give too much importance to early experiences which are the basis of perception of all future experiences.
- The habitus is a product of history and produces individual and collective practices (which in turn make more history and generate schemes).
	- Past experiences decide which practices are 'correct' and 'normal', more reliable than explicit rules.
	- Reactivates in similar structures, causing continuity.
- The habitus enables ONLY the free production of all thoughts, perceptions and actions as allowed by the conditions it was produced and acquired in.
	- Infinite yet restricted.
	- Generates [[Y1 T1 SOC Society/CONCEPTS/Commonsense\|Commonsense]] behaviours and excludes negatively sanctioned behaviours.
- Influence of past learned behaviour is not felt because they are deeply rooted in the unconscious ([[Y1 T1 SOC Society/PEOPLE/Émile Durkheim\|Émile Durkheim]]).
- The habitus = embodied history, internalized as second nature of an individual. 
	- Brings together objectifications of history: in bodies and in institutions (incorporated and objectified capital, as explained in [[Y1 T1 SOC Society/READINGS/PRIMARY/SOC3.08 Bourdieu, The Forms of Capital\|SOC3.08 Bourdieu, The Forms of Capital]]).
- Contributes to adding stimuli to the modus operandi of which it is the product. 
 - The habitus allows institutions to be inhabited by agents who partake of the history objectified in institutions.
 - This makes it possible for institutions to function: when agents readily believe in social performances, they gain roles that come with privileges and obligations, which set up the stucture of a society.
 - A commonsense world with objective meanings and practices is created by consensus and harmonization of habitus. 
	 - Makes phenomena immediately understandable and foreseeable.
	 - Practices believed to be normal are believed to be automatic and impersonal.
- Leads to homogenization of group/class habitus due to homogeneity of conditions of existence, without explicit coordination among agents.
- Habitus = immanent law (lex insita), precondition for coordination of practice and practices of coordination.  
- Efforts at mobilization have to deal with differing habitus of agents.
	- Hysteresis of habitus: mismatch between an individual's embodied dispositions and their environment, leading to missed opportunities.

- Social class: class of individuals having identical or similar conditions of existence.
- Relationship between class habitus and individual habitus: individual reflects the class, and is a structural variant of the class.
- Each individual habitus differs in social trajectory.
- Early experiences of individuals have particular weight because the habitus tends to remain constant and defend against change. 
- Aspirations shaped by what is accessible and inaccessible, adjusting to the objective chances of satisfying need or desire.
