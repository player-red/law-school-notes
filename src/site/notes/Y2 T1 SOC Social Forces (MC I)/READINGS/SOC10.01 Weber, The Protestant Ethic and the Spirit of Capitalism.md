Introduction to the The Protestant Ethic and the Spirit of Capitalism, by [[Y2 T1 SOC Social Forces (MC I)/PEOPLE/Max Weber\|Max Weber]].
- Rationalizing tendencies in Western societies and how ideas shape them.
- Weber saw religious beliefs as systems of meaning to explain the world.
- Protestants had a calling and had to work towards it as part of their goal to submit to God. 
	- Discouraged economic profiteering by insisting that each individual should be content with their own lot, since it was given to them by God.
- Changed in the hands of Calvin and Baxter.
	- Interpreted this as a command to ==work== for God's glory.
	- A sign of fulfilling this command was economic prosperity. Profit became a visible blessing from God, to be used again for work and not idle time/luxury. Saving and investing was highly encouraged.

The Spirit of Capitalism
- Quotes concepts of time being money, and that money is able to multiply itself if used wisely.
- Describes these attitudes as utilitarianism, but even a mere farce would suffice for those values to be fulfilled. 
	- The values are only useful till they wield some utility for the individual, and if a farce yields utility then it works the same as the actual quality. 
- Utility is described as an individual practicing righteousness.
- Why should a man earn money? — Biblical belief of a diligent man rising to the top and standing before kings.
- An individual now born into the capitalist system is forced to work within it and conform to its rules.
- Calls [[Y1 T1 SOC Society/PEOPLE/Karl Marx\|Karl Marx]]'s historical materialism naive, disagrees with his idea that the ideas of working hard/time is money/etc originate due to the base economic structure as part of the superstructure.
- Argues that this mindset would be one considered greedy and barbaric in the Middle Ages, not because the greed for gold was less.
- Employers use piece rates to force employees to think about opportunity cost and give them incentive to work more, but this is countered by man's will to earn only as much as enables him to live comfortably.
- Lowering wages to force labourers to work more: doesn't work beyond a certain point, fails when skilled labour/expensive machinery required. 
- If doing labour was considered divinely ordained, working as much as possible to earn as much as possible would be considered a virtue.

Asceticism and the Spirit of Capitalism
- Richard Baxter: Puritan ethics.
	- Presbyterian who moved away from pure Calvinism.
	- Considered wealth morally suspect since it tempts people, unlike Calvin who saw it as a means to increase the prestige of the clergy. 
	- The real objection to wealth was idleness that arose from using it for relaxation and enjoyment, not the acquisition of wealth itself.
	- Considered working and activity as a means to honour God. Hence, wasting time was to commit a sin. 
	- Labour was approved of as an ascetic technique, prescribed to everyone including the wealthy.
	- Division of labour considered to be a part of the divine scheme of things. 
- Some believed wealth had a secularizing influence, decaying religious values by increasing pride and greed. 
- Ascetic values being used widely in the real world have now created an iron cage.
