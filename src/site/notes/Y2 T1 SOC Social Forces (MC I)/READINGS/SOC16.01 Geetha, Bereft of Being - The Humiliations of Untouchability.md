Chapter 5 in Humiliation: Claims and Context. pp. 95-107.

About Humiliation
- Humiliation: an experience that interrogates and recasts one's relationship to oneself.
- When a person's existence itself is proof of their lowness, they suffer a crisis considered an ontological wounding.
- Degrading or hurting someone else can only be done if you can exercise power over them. 
- Forces the body to bear witness against itself. 

On Untouchability
- [[Y1 T1 SOC Society/PEOPLE/Dr B. R. Ambedkar\|Dr B. R. Ambedkar]]'s idea of graded inequality, norm that informs the caste system. 
- The existence of people considered untouchable is itself taboo and they are subjected to continuous humiliation because the system requires it. 

The Curse of Labour
- Dalits experience extreme alienation since their work is considered not to have any value (symbolic or material) because of their low caste.
- Everyday humiliation suffered: food taboos, water use, clothes, etc.
- Dalits cannot claim the right to knowledge since it is reserved for Brahmins, who are considered to produced knowledge that is independent of labour.
- [[Y1 T1 SOC Society/CONCEPTS/Othering\|Othering]] of Dalits by considering them expendable and separate beings.

The Impossibility of Love
- Upper caste men are excused for their virility and upper caste women are expected to be chaste.
- Lower caste men and women are seen as promiscuous and impure.

Untouchable Angst
- Constantly have to bear the stigma of dealing with what the body rejects and pollution. 
The Impossibility of History
- Enquiries into the origin of pollution or purity are seen as transgressions.
- Search for a past that Dalits can make their own.
The Body of Knowledge
- Injustice became a holy act sanctioned by god. 
- Challenging the caste order requires a commitment to challenging history.
