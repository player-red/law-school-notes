Of Cocks and Men
- Cockfights barely studied despite being a very popular pastime.
- Balinese has the same double entendre: see them as masculine symbols of excellence, in line with the Balinese conception of the body as a set of separately animated parts: the cocks are viewed as 'detachable, self-operating penises with a life of their own'.
- All types of fights are compared to cockfights, and the men involved in them to cocks.
- Island itself seen as a cock ready to challenge Java.
- Balinese men spend a lot of time getting their birds ready for cockfights.
- Argues that they are symbolic expressions of the male ego and virility, and animality, the Balinese idea of the exact opposite of humanity.
- Revulsion to animal-like behaviour: babies not allowed to crawl, demons represented by animal forms, puberty rite is filing child's teeth so they don't look like animals, defecation and eating regarded as disgusting (to be done quickly and in private).
	- Other than few domestic animals, aversive to animals.
- In identifying with the fighter cock, the Balinese man not only identifies with his masculinity (ideal self) but also 'powers of darkness', his fears and what he hates.
- A cockfight is a blood sacrifice to sate the demons' hunger.
	- Day of Silence (everyone sits silent to avoid influx of demons) is preceded by large-scale cockfights.

The Fight
- Spurs attached to the feet (sharp, pointed swords) by a small proportion of men who know how to do it properly, many rules and rites to take care of the spurs.
- Coconut pierced with a small hole acts as a timer during which handlers are not allowed to touch the roosters, after which they may.
- Fight begins, blows landed, handlers pick them up immediately after one.
- Coconut must sink three times after the cock which landed the blow must be set down to show he is firm, coconut sinks twice again and match starts again.
- Decision of the man who manages the coconut is absolute, the people never complain about it.
- Creates what is called a focused gathering by Goffman, a set of people engrossed in a common flow of activity and relating to each other in terms of that flow.
- Before Dutch invasion, cockfights were a compulsory duty for a man. 

Odds and Even Money
- Betting of two types: center and peripheral, center is always even and peripheral is never even. 
- All bets are immediately paid, this rule is never disputed.
- Evening out the center bet is done with the aim of making a deep/interesting match, where the outcome is as unpredictable as possible.

Playing with Fire
- Bentham's 'deep play': stakes so high that it would be irrational to participate in such a game, and he saw this as something to be prevented legally.
- To people engaging in such behaviour, money is less a measure of utility than it is a symbol of 'moral import': honor, esteem, dignity, STATUS. 
	- Smaller, more shallow fights will see people who are in it for the money.
	- Larger, higher stakes fights have 'true' enthusiasts.
- Balinese status hierarchy is then found in the cockfight, which becomes a simulation of the social matrix.
- Cockfights are seen as "playing with fire but not getting burned" by villagers: interpersonal and intergroup aggressions play out (something that almost never happens otherwise) but not exactly since it is "only a cockfight".

General principles
The more a match is:
1. Between near status equals (or personal enemies)
2. Between high status individuals
The deeper the match.

The deeper the match:
1. The closer the identification of the cock and man.
2. The finer the cocks involved and the more exact the matching.
3. The greater the emotion and general absorption in the match.
4. The higher the individual bets center and outside, shorter the outside bet odds and more betting overall.
5. The less an economic and more status view of the match involved.

Legend of a prince who was away cockfighting when his entire family was assassinated by commoner usurpers.

Feathers, Blood, Crowds, and Money
- No actual change in status due to cockfights.
- Cockfights are thus an art form that presents all those themes (of masculinity, status, pride, rage, loss, etc.) in a comprehensible and meaningful way. 
- Give some meaningful events to days where the Balinese feel like nothing much is happening (ebb and flow of the Balinese perception of life and time).
- Allows for the expression of pride and other 'wild' emotions in a socially acceptable way, in an otherwise controlled and subdued society.
- Metasocial commentary on the hierarchical ranking of Balinese society and then organizing the major part of collective existence around it, a Balinese reading of the Balinese experience.
