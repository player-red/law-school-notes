Chapter 1 in The Rules of Sociological Method, p. 50-59, by [[Y1 T1 SOC Society/PEOPLE/Émile Durkheim\|Émile Durkheim]].

- Every individual is subject to a certain set of beliefs and obligations that Durkheim argues exists outside themselves.
	- They are created by law and custom.
	- Propagated by education.
- These beliefs have a coercive power that enables them to impose themselves upon individuals.
	- They can cause an individual to conform to them, by their own belief or by some penalty (legal, social, monetary) or public conscience.
- Consist of manners of acting, thinking and feeling external to the individual, are not organic or psychical.
	- Hence, they are social <- interesting jump in reasoning. 
	- Argues this because if the individual is not the medium, society must be. 
- Social currents: collective manifestation that exerts pressure on the individual if they try to resist them. 
	- We are unaware of them if we already conform to them.
- Believes that everyone is under an illusion that they themselves are producing things that are imposed upon them externally.
	- Compares social currents to air, as in they have weight but we are unable to feel it. 
- Assume different forms through individuals but can be separated using statistics.
- Ways of functioning vs ways of being: political divisions in society, civic relationships, clusters of people in cities, architectural styles, communication networks.
