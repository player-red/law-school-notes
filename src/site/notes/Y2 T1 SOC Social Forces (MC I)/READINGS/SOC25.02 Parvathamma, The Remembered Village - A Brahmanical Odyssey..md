Critique of [[Y2 T1 SOC Social Forces (MC I)/READINGS/SOC25.01 Srinivas, The Social Structure of a Mysore Village\|SOC25.01 Srinivas, The Social Structure of a Mysore Village]], pp. 91-96. [[Y1 T1 SOC Society/CONCEPTS/Caste\|Caste]]

- Brahmin - non-Brahmin - Harijan hierarchy, but Harijans not discussed, since they are traditionally considered avarna, outside chaturvarna. 
- Women rank inferior to men in all social groups.
- Srinivas' view that Brahmin women must follow values (chastity, modesty, obedience, abstinence) and the further you go down the hierarchy the less stringent it becomes.
	- Superficial view of women, expectations placed on non-Brahmin ignored.
- Theoretical ideal of Brahmin superiority -> makes Srinivas a traditionalist.

- Brahmins quickly adapted to Westernization in order to preserve their privilege.
	- Not as easy for the illiterate non-Brahmins, excluded from education. 
- Describes the Brahmins as leaders of both Sanskritization and Westernization.

- Srinivas tried to establish the concept of a dominant caste (if not too low in the hierarchy) which is supported by a large number of people, material wealth, outside connections, etc. 
- Dominance and solidarity opposed to the theories of Sanskritization and Westernization: is dominance required for the latter theories? 
	- He describes them as independent processes, but shows no indication of intersection though they do.
