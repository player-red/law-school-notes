Chapter 10 of An Anthropologist among the Historians and Other Essays (pg. 224-254)

I
- Effects of colonial rule mainly felt when political and economic innovations used by colonizers to control products of colonized labour.
- Structural changes in colonial societies have been discussed, but not changes in culture.
- Culture: "a historically transmitted pattern of meanings embodied in symbols, a system of inherited conceptions expressed in forms by means of which men communicate, perpetuate and develop their knowledge about attitudes towards life" <- modern meaning.
	- Older meanings believe that it is a way of life.
- Historians view cultural change due to colonialism in terms of intellectual history.
	- The idea of the 'Bengal Renaissance' itself, Westernization, the replacement of Indian symbols with Western ones, etc.
- Elite Indians believed they were living through a cultural rebirth, which is a very Western idea based on European history.
- Effort to purify Hinduism to make it consistent with European ideas of rationality, empiricism, monotheism and individuality. 
- Led to ambivalence between East and West, perceived as hypocrisy.
- Tried to counter Western cultural imperialism by rediscovering their Indian cultural roots.
- Gita seen as the single most authoritative expression of Hinduism due to nationalist thought.

II The Pre-History of the Census
- British colonial government tried to collect information about Indian society and economy.
- First concerned with revenue collection by Indian tax collectors: gave them a systematic method of collecting revenue.
- Map-making, exploration, topographic surveys, etc.
- Hamilton created a Gazetteer that included history, latitude and longitude, population estimates, etc.
Early Attempts At Population Estimates and the Taking of Censuses
- Population of many cities overestimated due to:
	- Trying to enhance position by local rulers.
	- Perception of Europeans: commercial areas had narrow lanes and paths, creating an illusion of overcrowded streets that they were not used to.
- Trying to estimate the numbers using types of houses or families, sometimes based on profession as well.
- Underreporting number of women. 
- Family defined as either common expenditure or eating at the same hearth.
- Christian believed 'agriculturist' was defined too broadly, and his solution was to record the caste and profession of the head of each family.
- Tendency to report all of the same profession as of the same caste, though it was known that not all Brahmans worked as priests.
Development of the Modern Census
- Postponed to 1871 due to the Revolt of 1857 and general feelings of interference in the lives of Indians.
- Not consistent across provinces, including definitions of heads of households, literacy rates, towns and villages, etc. and little cooperation from the population.
- Where an Indian ruler would have been able to promote or demote castes in their jurisdiction, British officials could instead.
- Views on the caste system were mainly based on data from the census.
- Reorganizing military based on 'martial races', trying to find out which castes were organizing against the British, which ones were hoarding power and opportunities: bias already crept in when caste data was being collected.
- Tried to place jatis within the varna system, or in blocks.
- Failed due to the absence of a uniform system of classification. 
- In order to standardize classification, standard names and variations were assigned to various castes. 
- Aggregation of castes led to more problems: separation of castes into 'castes of good social position' and 'inferior castes'.
