- Each social group which has an essential function in economic production creates a strata of intellectuals.
- This gives the group awareness of its function (in economic, social and political fields) and homogeneity.

- Elite intellectuals must be able to organize society in some sense, including the state. 
- This must be done in order to expand their class.
- "Organic intellectuals" created by each class are specializations of the activities that the social class has brought into prominence.

- Peasantry does not create its own organic intellectuals or have traditional intellectuals, despite many such intellectuals being of peasant origin.

- Finding one criterion to characterise all activities done by intellectuals: should not be looking at the nature of these activities, rather in the ==systems of relations== in which these activities take place. 
- All men are intellectuals, but not all men have the function of intellectuals in society. 
	- The difference between intellectuals and non-intellectuals refers to this difference in social function. 
	- No human activity exists in which some intellectual capacity is not used. 

- Argues the "new intellectual" needs to participate actively in practical life rather than give eloquent speeches (traditional idea of the academic).

- Intellectuals are elaborated through schools and the complexity of their intellectual function is measured objectively by the number and gradation of specialized schools.

- Civil society and political society/State = hegemony and direct domination respectively. 
- Intellectuals act as deputies of the dominant groups, and exercise functions for both:
	- Direction imposed by dominant group obtains "spontaneous consent" of the citizens due to their prestige derived from position and function in the world of production. 
	- Apparatus of state power, used when consent has failed, to legally enforce discipline on those who do not consent.
