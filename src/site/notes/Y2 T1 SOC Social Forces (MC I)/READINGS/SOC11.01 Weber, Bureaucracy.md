By [[Y2 T1 SOC Social Forces (MC I)/PEOPLE/Max Weber\|Max Weber]], explaining [[Y2 T1 SOC Social Forces (MC I)/CONCEPTS/Rationalization\|Rationalization]].

Characteristics of Modern Bureaucracy
- Jurisdictional areas ordered by rules:
	- Regular activities required for governance: official duties.
	- Authority to give commands for discharge of duties: distributed and delimited by rules.
	- Provision made for regular and continuous fulfillment of duties. Only people who qualify under general rules are employed.
- Three elements together make a bureaucratic agency in a State, and a bureaucratic enterprise in the private sphere.

- Office hierarchy and channels of appeal create a system of super- and subordination where the higher offices supervise the lower ones.
- Office hierarchy is monocratically organized. 

- System managed using documents (files).
- "The body of officials working in an agency along with the respective apparatus of material implements and the files makes up a bureau." 
- Public money and property separated from the private property of the official. 

- Office management generally requires training in a specialized field.
- Official activity demands the full working capacity of the official.
- Management of the office follows general rules which are stable, exhaustive and can be learned. 
	- Contrast to organizations in which relationships were regulated by individual privileges and favour.

The Position of the Official within and outside of the Bureaucracy
I. Office holding as a vocation
- Special examinations are often used to appoint officials, who generally undergo a training course.
- Entrance to an office is considered acceptance of a specific duty, rather than work in exchange for money/perks.
	- Does not establish a relationship to a person.
	- For impersonal and functional purposes.

II. The social position of the official
- Social esteem and status convention: generally has a higher status than the governed. Higher in countries where officials are generally from privileged backgrounds due to high cost of training (time, money, etc).
- Appointment versus election: consequences for expertise: typically appointed by a superior authority, since an official elected by a governed is not purely a bureaucratic official. 
	- Designation by election would have an effect on hierarchical subordination: superiors would not be the ones in power, rather the governed, which would defeat the hierarchical system. 
	- Weber believes an appointed official is selected purely based on functional points.
	- Hence, he believes popular election would endanger large administrative bodies which are difficult to supervise.
- Tenure and the inverse relationship between judicial independence and social prestige: tenure for life is assumed but is not a proprietary right to ensure impersonal discharge of duties.
- Rank as the basis of regular salary: monetary compensation has two parts - salary and pension (old age security). Amount determined by status/rank and length of service. 
- Fixed career lines and status rigidity: moving upwards from lower rank to higher rank, through seniority and grades in exams.

The Technical Superiority of Bureaucratic Organization over Administration by Notables
- Precision, speed, unambiguity, impersonality, strict subordination, etc.
- Objective discharge of business without personal bias or 'regard for persons'.
- Calculable rules = calculable results.
- *Sine ira et studio*: without anger and passion.
- Praised by capitalism because it is dehumanized and impersonal.

The Leveling of Social Differences
- Administrative Democratization
	- Leveling economic and social differences.
	- A result of the demand for equality before the law in a mass democracy. 
	- Signifies increased cashflow, since in the past the cheapest way of fulfilling the need for administration was leaving it to rich landlords and nobles.
- Mass parties and the bureaucratic consequences of democratization
	- Bureaucratization within state administration develops in parallel to democracy. 
	- Democracy seeks to shorten terms and not limit election to individuals with special qualifications: exact opposite of the goals of the bureaucracy. 
- The Objective and Subjective Bases of Bureaucratic Perpetuity
	- Hardest social structure to destroy. 
	- Powerful instrument for those who control bureaucratic apparatus. 
	- Individual bureaucrat chained to his duty.
	- Ruled cannot replace the apparatus since it is made up of expert training, and trying to do so would result in chaos.
