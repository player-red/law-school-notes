Part III in Mind, Self, and Society.

- Self is different from the physiological organism. 
- Not there at birth but develops with social experience and an individual's relation to the process of development + other individuals.

- Body can exist without a self being involved. 
	- Direct opposite of what Fanon argues in [[Y2 T1 SOC Social Forces (MC I)/READINGS/SOC15.01 Fanon, The Fact of Blackness\|SOC15.01 Fanon, The Fact of Blackness]].
- The self is an "object to itself" and can be distinguished from the rest of the body. 
- Bodily experiences are organized about a self, distinguishable from the self: can lose parts of the body without any serious invasion of the self.
	- Greatly disagree: what about people who have had amputations? Phantom limb syndrome? Even a significant (permanent) scar left on the body changes one's perception of oneself.
- The body does not experience itself as a whole. Says it is similar to feeling a table, a physical sensation exists. 

- The self is ==reflexive==, can be both subject and object. 
- It is conscious = experience of one's self. 
- If one is completely occupied in an activity, heightened focus may make them lose consciousness of self, self as an object does not enter such experiences. 
- The individual becomes an object to himself by taking the attitudes of others towards himself in a social environment.
- Communication is a form of behaviour that allows the individual to become an object to himself.
	- Communication through symbols, behaviour, etc. (symbolic interactionism): individuals may interpret meaning without realizing.
- Hence, the self is a social structure and rises in a social experience. 
- Mead says it is impossible to conceive a self outside of a social experience.
- "Conversation of gestures": symbolic interactionism. 
- It is possible to speak to yourself as you would to another person, but one would inevitably seek out an audience. 
- The self changes as per the context and audience. Each of these selves represents an aspect of the complete self.
	- The complete self is a reflection of the complete social process.
- Children's pretend play: taking on different roles, using stimuli and responses and organizes them into a whole. 
	- Different roles have definite relationships with each other. 
	- Each child is ready to take the role of everyone else.
	- The acts of each child are determined by assumptions as to how every other child will act. 
	- "Generalized other": organized community or social group that gives the individual his unity of self.
	- A complete sense of self can only develop if the attitudes of the organized social group are taken. 
	- An individual can only think if he takes the attitude of the generalized other towards himself. 

- Full development of self: attitudes of other individuals + attitudes of generalized other towards him. 
- A person takes the language of a community as a medium through which a self is obtained, and then takes on the different roles of a community to get the different attitudes of the members of the community. 
- Selves can only exist in definite relations to other selves.
- No line can be drawn between our own selves and the selves of others, because both are dependent on each other for existing.
