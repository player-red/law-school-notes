By [[Y1 T1 SOC Society/PEOPLE/Émile Durkheim\|Émile Durkheim]], in Sociological Theory in the Classical Era: Text and Readings, 3rd edition.
/
- Argues that a society is a power controlling individuals and that it performs a regulatory function, which can affect the suicide rate in a country. 
I
- If suicide rates increase during times of crisis, he argues that they should decrease when comfort levels increase. 
- Finds that poverty cannot be linked to increasing rates of suicide and nor can industrial or financial crises.
- Argues that this is due to disturbance of collective order instead.
- Readjustments in social order lead to self-destruction.

II
- No one can exist in happiness if their needs are not met. 
- Having needs left unmet reduces the will to live. 
- Animals only depend on material conditions and continue living, but the same cannot be said for humans. 
- The more unattainable wants exist, the more unhappy an individual becomes.
- Since there is no way for an individual to limit their own ambitions, society must step in and act as a regulatory mechanism as a moral force.
- Makes an interesting argument about heredity and the inherent inequality in economic positions assigned at birth: says that if inheritances are abolished, everyone would begin life with equal resources and would feel that life is as it should be.
	- Would [[Y1 T1 SOC Society/PEOPLE/Karl Marx\|Karl Marx]] agree?
	- Would [[Y1 T1 SOC Society/PEOPLE/Pierre Bourdieu\|Pierre Bourdieu]] agree? The advantage provided by economic capital in such a world would not exist, but what about social and cultural capital?
		- Durkheim goes on to talk about "natural talent" received at birth, that it would exist even with economic equality. 
- Regulating power must also have the backing of respect and not fear.

III
- Anomie: lack of social or ethical standards in an individual or a group.
- Economic anomie: complete change in economic order due to the modern capitalism. 
	- Producers now have the entire world as potential customers, leading to growing ambitions on both sides. 
- Anomic suicide: caused due to man's activity lacking regulation and suffering caused due to said lack.
- Egoistic suicide: no basis for existence in life.
- Altruistic suicide: basis for existence is beyond life itself. 

IV
- Types of anomic suicide:
	- Economic
	- Domestic: death of spouse, family catastrophe, etc.
