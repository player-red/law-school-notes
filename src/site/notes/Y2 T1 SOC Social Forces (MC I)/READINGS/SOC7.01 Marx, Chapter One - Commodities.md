In Capital: Volume 1.
Maybe we have to let go of the idea of expecting something in return for doing something for someone else for communism to function without exploitation?

Section I: The Two Factors of a Commodity: Use-value and Value (The Substance of Value and the Magnitude of Value)
- Capitalist mode of production: accumulation of commodities.
- Commodity: a thing that satisfies human wants by its properties.
- Utility: value of using the thing, limited by physical properties of the commodity, does not exist outside of it.
	- Independent of labour required to produce the commodity.
	- Only becomes a reality by use/consumption.
- Exchange value: at first glance a quantitative relation. 
	- Appears to be relative intrinsic value of commodities.
- All commodities have one fact in common, that they are products of labour, and in the modern world, all commodities have been reduced to the same sort of labour. (eg. Ford factory production, specialization)
	- Thus, values are common to them all, since human labour is embodied in all commodities.
- Magnitude of value measured by amount of labour that is socially necessary, which is the value creating substance.
- Two commodities that have the same quantity of labour embodied in them should have the same value, if the labour time is consistent.
- Labour time is a variable since labour productivity is never constant.
- Value of a commodity directly proportional to quantity of labour, inversely proportional to productiveness of labour.
- A commodity or thing can have use value without having social exchange value (air, water).

Section 4: The Fetishism of Commodities and the Secret Thereof
- A commodity transcends the materials it is made of.
	- Due to labour and time taken to exercise it.
	- Social form of labour is created when commodities are made for other human beings, to exchange.
- Mutual relations of producers create social relations between their products.
- Commodities when produced by labour, and ==fetishized== (entering into relations with each other and the human race).
- Aggregate labour of society: sum total of labour of private individuals.
- Social character of products not visible till the act of exchange, since labour does not come in contact with other labour till exchange.
- Labour has a two-fold character:
	- Must satisfy the want of another.
	- Capable of satisfying the wants of the labourer, if it can satisfy the want of another.
- Equalisation of labour occurs only when it is reduced to its common denominator or when their inherent inequalities are ignored.
- By equating the exchange value of commodities, we equate different types of human labour.
- Social relations appear as mutual personal relations due to personal dependence on the labour of others.
- Communist society: social labour instead of individual - your produce must be used by yourself and others.
- Religion reflects the real world, and the reflex can only disappear when man is working with each other and in conjunction with nature.
- Social values control man rather than be controlled by him.
- Use value can be realized without exchange, through direct relations between the objects and man. But exchange value is only realized by social process.
