Chapters 1 and 2.

Chapter 1
What a constitution is
- Describing the system of government in a country, partially legal and partially non-legal (customs, understandings, etc) in order to regulate the government. (Britain)
- Other countries: consolidated document containing generally only legal rules which governs the government of that country. 
- Supplemented by legal rules enacted by the legislature = organic laws.

Chapter 2
How constitutions may be classified
- Old classification: written and unwritten, should be discarded since no country truly has an unwritten Constitution. 
- Can be classified according to the method of amendment. 
	- Amended by legislature through the same process as any other law.
	- Amended by legislature by a special process.
- Creates a spectrum of flexible to rigid constitutions.
- The ease or the frequency of the amendments also depends on the dominant social and political groups in the community. 
- Can also be divided into Constitutions that are supreme over the legislature and those which are not.
- Another classification: central vs. local governments (federal/unitary/confederate distinction).
- Method by which power is distributed in a government: those that use the doctrine of separation of power and those that do not.
- Another old classification: republican vs monarchical
